Our evidence standard

We only use these kinds of evidence. We never cite blogs, coaching websites, videos or opinion pieces.

  • Systematic review Researchers found and assessed every good study on a question, then summarised them.
  • Meta-analysis A systematic review that also pools the numbers from many studies for one overall answer.
  • RCT Randomised controlled trial: teams or players are split at random, so differences in results come from the programme itself.
  • Consensus statement Leading experts from recognised bodies (such as the IOC or the Concussion in Sport Group) review the evidence and agree on recommendations.
  • Federation framework Official published guidance from a national football federation, such as match formats for each age.
  • Coaching principle A coaching method (like a particular drill) that hasn’t been trialled on its own. We cite the higher-level evidence for the principle behind it, such as game-based practice, and never present the drill itself as tested.

CoachHacks gives general education, not medical advice. If a player is hurt or unwell, see a doctor or physiotherapist.

Injury prevention

Emery et al., 2015 Meta-analysis

Systematic review and meta-analysis: neuromuscular training reduced lower-limb injury risk in youth sport by about a third (IRR 0.64); getting programmes actually used is the main challenge.

Emery CA, Roy TO, Whittaker JL, Nettel-Aguirre A, van Mechelen W (2015). Neuromuscular training injury prevention strategies in youth sport: a systematic review and meta-analysis. British Journal of Sports Medicine 49(13):865-870.

Read the paper

Harøy et al., 2019 RCT

Cluster RCT in male footballers: a simple adductor (Copenhagen) strengthening programme lowered the risk of groin problems by 41%.

Harøy J, Clarsen B, Wiger EG, Øyen MG, Serner A, Thorborg K, et al. (2019). The Adductor Strengthening Programme prevents groin problems among male football players: a cluster-randomised controlled trial. British Journal of Sports Medicine 53(3):150-157.

Read the paper

Lauersen et al., 2014 Meta-analysis

Meta-analysis of 25 trials (26,610 people): strength training cut sports injuries to about a third (RR 0.32); stretching showed no protective effect.

Lauersen JB, Bertelsen DM, Andersen LB (2014). The effectiveness of exercise interventions to prevent sports injuries: a systematic review and meta-analysis of randomised controlled trials. British Journal of Sports Medicine 48(11):871-877.

Read the paper

Lloyd et al., 2014 Consensus statement

International consensus: supervised, age-appropriate resistance training is safe and beneficial for children and adolescents, and supports a long-term approach to building strength.

Lloyd RS, Faigenbaum AD, Stone MH, Oliver JL, Jeffreys I, Moody JA, et al. (2014). Position statement on youth resistance training: the 2014 International Consensus. British Journal of Sports Medicine 48(7):498-505.

Read the paper

Rössler et al., 2018 RCT

Multinational cluster RCT in 3,895 players aged 7–13: an age-specific warm-up cut injuries by 48% and severe injuries by 74%; the more often teams did it, the bigger the effect.

Rössler R, Junge A, Bizzini M, Verhagen E, Chomiak J, aus der Fünten K, et al. (2018). A Multinational Cluster Randomised Controlled Trial to Assess the Efficacy of ‘11+ Kids’: A Warm-Up Programme to Prevent Injuries in Children’s Football. Sports Medicine 48(6):1493-1504.

Read the paper

Soligard et al., 2008 RCT

Cluster RCT in young female footballers (13–17): a structured warm-up reduced injuries overall, and severe and overuse injuries by about half.

Soligard T, Myklebust G, Steffen K, Holme I, Silvers H, Bizzini M, et al. (2008). Comprehensive warm-up programme to prevent injuries in young female footballers: cluster randomised controlled trial. BMJ 337:a2469.

Read the paper

Steib et al., 2017 Meta-analysis

Meta-analysis of 16 trials in youth athletes: neuromuscular training cut injuries by 42%; 10–15 minute sessions two or three times a week worked as well as longer ones.

Steib S, Rahlf AL, Pfeifer K, Zech A (2017). Dose-Response Relationship of Neuromuscular Training for Injury Prevention in Youth Athletes: A Meta-Analysis. Frontiers in Physiology 8.

Read the paper

Thorborg et al., 2017 Meta-analysis

Meta-analysis of cluster RCTs in recreational football: structured exercise-based warm-up programmes reduced overall injury rates (the 11+ type by 39%).

Thorborg K, Krommes KK, Esteve E, Clausen MB, Bartels EM, Rathleff MS (2017). Effect of specific exercise-based football injury prevention programmes on the overall injury rate in football: a systematic review and meta-analysis of the FIFA 11 and 11+ programmes. British Journal of Sports Medicine 51(7):562-571.

Read the paper

Van der Horst et al., 2015 RCT

RCT in 579 amateur footballers: adding the Nordic hamstring exercise to regular training significantly reduced hamstring injuries (OR 0.28).

van der Horst N, Smits DW, Petersen J, Goedhart EA, Backx FJ (2015). The Preventive Effect of the Nordic Hamstring Exercise on Hamstring Injuries in Amateur Soccer Players. The American Journal of Sports Medicine 43(6):1316-1323.

Read the paper

Van Dyk et al., 2019 Meta-analysis

Meta-analysis of 8,459 athletes: programmes that include the Nordic hamstring exercise reduce hamstring injuries by up to 51%.

van Dyk N, Behan FP, Whiteley R (2019). Including the Nordic hamstring exercise in injury prevention programmes halves the rate of hamstring injuries: a systematic review and meta-analysis of 8459 athletes. British Journal of Sports Medicine 53(21):1362-1370.

Read the paper

Concussion

Davis et al., 2023 Consensus statement

The Child SCAT6: the consensus assessment tool for health professionals evaluating suspected concussion in children aged 8–12.

Davis GA, Echemendia RJ, Ahmed OH, Anderson V, Blauwet C, Brett BL, et al. (2023). Introducing the Child Sport Concussion Assessment Tool 6 (Child SCAT6). British Journal of Sports Medicine 57(11):632-635.

Read the paper

Echemendia et al., 2023 Consensus statement

The Concussion Recognition Tool 6 (CRT6): helps people without medical training recognise a suspected concussion and remove the player from play.

Echemendia RJ, Ahmed OH, Bailey CM, Bruce JM, Burma JS, Davis GA, et al. (2023). Introducing the Concussion Recognition Tool 6 (CRT6). British Journal of Sports Medicine 57(11):689-691.

Read the paper

Patricios et al., 2023 Consensus statement

6th International Consensus on Concussion in Sport (Amsterdam 2022): current recognition, removal, assessment and return-to-sport guidance, and the CRT6 and SCAT6 tools.

Patricios JS, Schneider KJ, Dvorak J, Ahmed OH, Blauwet C, Cantu RC, et al. (2023). Consensus statement on concussion in sport: the 6th International Conference on Concussion in Sport–Amsterdam, October 2022. British Journal of Sports Medicine 57(11):695-711.

Read the paper

Youth development

Bergeron et al., 2015 Consensus statement

IOC consensus on youth athletic development: prioritise health, enjoyment and inclusive participation, account for growth and maturation, and avoid early specialisation.

Bergeron MF, Mountjoy M, Armstrong N, Chia M, Côté J, Emery CA, et al. (2015). International Olympic Committee consensus statement on youth athletic development. British Journal of Sports Medicine 49(13):843-851.

Read the paper

Lloyd et al., 2016 Consensus statement

NSCA position statement on long-term athletic development: structured, age-appropriate physical preparation (including strength) through childhood and adolescence, adjusted for growth and maturity.

Lloyd RS, Cronin JB, Faigenbaum AD, Haff GG, Howard R, Kraemer WJ, et al. (2016). National Strength and Conditioning Association Position Statement on Long-Term Athletic Development. Journal of Strength and Conditioning Research 30(6):1491-1509.
